    warned about exactly once rather than once per request;

  - a cookie the server cannot judge (the JWT secret itself unreachable):

    so loopback gating fails closed instead of throwing out of the hook;
  - HSTS present on https, absent on http, other defaults unchanged either way.

vi.setSystemTime either side of it rather than measured. 6 tests, all passing.

(d) A PRE-EXISTING RACE, reported and not fixed. The combined run's PostgreSQL
    leg failed one case: "C04 archive before claim and receipt before
    settlement > two concurrent preparations archive one member set and leave
    one claimable operation", with
    `duplicate key value violates unique constraint factory_release_operations_pkey`
    (SQLSTATE 23505). It passes in isolation (10 pass, 0 fail,
    archive-writer-retry.log) and the identical case has failed under load in at
    least three earlier packages' runs since 2026-09-14
    (w07-validation, w08-validation, w10, wave4a).

    The shape is precise, and the method is `prepare`, not `declare` — the
    earlier report named the statement rather than its method.
    factory_release_operations carries BOTH a primary key
    (tenant_id, project_id, operation_id) and a unique index
    idx_factory_release_operations_identity on the nine identity columns. The
    INSERT in FactoryReleases.declare (src/factory/releases.ts:771) writes
    `ON CONFLICT (tenant_id,project_id,run_id,node_instance_id,
    candidate_generation,action,destination_provider,destination_account,
    destination_object) DO NOTHING`, which infers the unique index as its sole
    arbiter. Two concurrent declares of the same operation can therefore
    conflict on the PRIMARY KEY instead, and a conflict on a non-arbiter
    constraint is RAISED, not suppressed. That line is not in this package's
    diff (its hunks are at +529, 980, 1014 and +1023), and fixing it would be a
    behaviour change outside a no-behaviour-change brief. Routed to whoever owns
    release authority, and W07c has since fixed it separately at that statement.

(e) The browser-route coverage receipt is CI-only and is therefore absent from
    every measurement above. Said plainly wherever it matters: the global floor,
    the per-file gate, and the full-repo CRAP ratchet all read a merge without
